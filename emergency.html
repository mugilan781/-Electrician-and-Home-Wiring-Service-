When to Call

Electrical Emergencies We Handle

Our emergency team is trained for all critical residential electrical situations. If in doubt, call us — we'd rather you call unnecessarily than delay in a real emergency.

Burning Smell or Sparks
Any burning smell from outlets, panels, or wiring is a potential fire hazard. Don't wait — call us and vacate the area immediately.
Complete Power Outage
Home-wide power loss not explained by a utility outage may indicate a main breaker failure or serious wiring fault.
Repeatedly Tripping Breakers
Breakers that won't stay reset indicate an overloaded or faulted circuit that could cause fire if left unaddressed.
Lightning or Storm Damage
Storm damage can overload or destroy your electrical system. Have your wiring inspected before restoring power after a severe storm.
Flickering or Buzzing Lights
Persistent flickering or buzzing can indicate loose wiring, overloaded circuits, or arcing — all serious fire hazards.
Flooded Electrical Systems
Never touch electrical systems in a flooded area. Our team can safely inspect and restore power after water intrusion.
Before Help Arrives

Stay Safe While
You Wait for Us

Following these steps while waiting for our emergency team helps protect you and your family and makes our job faster and safer when we arrive.

Call 911 first if there is any risk of fire, electrocution, or injury
Switch off the main breaker if safe to do so and evacuate
Do NOT touch sparking outlets or wiring with bare hands
Unplug appliances and electronic devices from affected circuits
Keep children and pets away from the affected area
Have your address ready and keep your phone line clear
